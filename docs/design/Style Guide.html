Chinese display — the hero font

Noto Serif SC at 700 weight is the default for any character display ≥ 24px. Its ink-trap modulation reads as "calligraphic" without becoming illegible at small sizes, and it ships every CJK glyph we'll need across HSK 2–5.

  • Hero / pack reveal: 96–160px, 700–900 weight
  • Card face: 64–96px depending on tier, 700
  • Inline / answer chip: 24–32px, 600
  • Thumbnail / list row: 16–18px, 500 — never below 16px
  • Pinyin (Latin extended): Noto Serif SC also covers tone marks; 500 weight, +6% tracking

05 — Primitives

Core UI

Every primitive is sized for thumb reach: 48px minimum touch height, generous tap targets, no fussy iconography. Buttons commit visually — primary is the warm cream of a flipped card, not a vivid brand color.

Buttons — primary & gold

Primary uses Paper for a tactile, "flipped card" feel. Gold appears at most once per screen — saving it for the rare-pull confirmation. Min height 48px.

Buttons — secondary & icon

Secondary is hairline-only — confident enough to be a real choice, quiet enough to defer. Icon buttons are a 48 × 48 elevated square, never floating without a surface.

Chips — radical & HSK level

06 — The core loop

Four-direction study

Prompt centered, four answers fanned to the cardinals. Distractors come from the same radical family, the same HSK level, or the player's own collection — never random. The whole board lives in the lower 75% of the screen so the thumb never reaches up.

Why a cross, not a 2×2 grid

The cardinal layout makes each answer feel like a direction the player commits to — closer to a fighting-game input than a quiz button. It also gives the prompt visual primacy (it's literally at the center of attention) and frees the corners for ambient state.

Thumb safety

The board occupies the lower ~75% of the viewport. Up/left/right/down answers all fall inside a 5cm thumb arc from a right-handed grip. The skip control sits bottom-right because dismissing should require a tiny extra commitment.

Distractor logic

Three of four distractors share the prompt's radical (here 氵). The fourth is a near-homophone or near-meaning — calibrated by the SRS engine so easy cards stay easy and shaky cards stay honest.

Feedback choreography

Correct: prompt and chosen answer both flash jade for 240ms, then dissolve. Incorrect: chosen answer flushes terracotta for 320ms, the right answer pulses jade once, prompt remains until the player taps to continue.

07 — Motion

Two tempos

Almost all motion lives in two registers. Common flip is the rhythm of study — fast, snappy, repeatable hundreds of times in a session. Rare reveal is ceremony — it interrupts the rhythm to mark a moment.
